Ask a company if AI is paying off and most say yes. Ask if they have checked, and the story changes. In KPMG's Q3 2026 AI Pulse (2,131 leaders), only **12%** consistently assess AI's value against its cost.

## 1. Measure before you switch it on

A Fortune 500 software firm rolled an AI assistant out to **5,179** support agents in stages, so it could compare agents with AI against agents without. Issues resolved per hour rose **14%** (NBER study).

## 2. Measure what the customer feels, not just speed

In a field experiment on Alibaba's Taobao (680,676 chats), an AI agent made chats faster, but customers rated those chats much lower. Klarna's CEO said the company had been "too focused on cost"; quality dropped and it started hiring human agents again.

## 3. Put cost and value side by side, per project

Companies with proven AI returns are **4x** more likely to do this (48% vs 12%, KPMG).

## 4. Back a few big bets

JPMorgan moved from counting use cases to about **50** strategic initiatives, each owned by a business team. Jamie Dimon says its AI spend now roughly pays for itself: about $2B of benefit for $2B of expense, company-reported.

## The takeaway

ROI is not worked out at the end. You plan it on day one: baseline, metrics, cost tracking.
