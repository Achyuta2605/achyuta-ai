Most AI projects do not fail because of the model. They fail because of the data feeding it. Gartner predicts that through 2026, companies will abandon **60% of AI projects** that lack AI-ready data ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)).

"AI-ready" does not mean "a lot of data". It means data that is scoped to a problem, owned by someone, checked every day, and described well enough that a person or a model can find the right table. Here is how four companies do it.

## 1. Pick one problem first

JPMorgan Chase builds small **data products**: each one is made for a single job and owned by the people who know that data best ([McKinsey interview with JPMorgan's CDO](https://mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/data-in-the-age-of-ai-a-conversation-with-mark-birkhead-of-jpmorgan-chase)). Instead of "clean the whole warehouse", the question becomes "what does this one use case need, and who is accountable for it?"

## 2. Give key data an owner and a stamp of approval

Airbnb's **Midas** process certifies its most important tables as the gold standard, so everyone builds on data they can trust ([Airbnb Engineering](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/data-quality-at-airbnb-870d03080469)). Certification is a promise: this table has an owner, a definition and quality checks.

## 3. Check data quality every day, automatically

Uber's **Data Quality Monitor** alerts the owner when numbers look off ([Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/en-NZ/blog/monitoring-data-quality-at-scale/)). The cost of skipping this is real: in 2022, bad data from one large customer helped cause a roughly **$110M** revenue hit at Unity ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2022/05/12/why-unity-software-tanked-39-this-week)).

## 4. Label your data

LinkedIn built **DataHub**, a catalog that shows what each dataset means, who owns it and where it came from ([LinkedIn Engineering](https://engineering.linkedin.com/blog/2019/data-hub)). That metadata is what lets people, and AI systems doing retrieval, find the right data instead of a stale copy.

## Bonus: AI can help with the clean-up

Walmart used LLMs to create or fix more than **850 million** product details in its catalog ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/walmart-generative-ai-data-experience/724426/)).

## The takeaway

Start with one problem, give its data an owner, monitor it daily and describe it. That is most of what "AI-ready" means in practice.
